How Prospector works

Prospector answers a question by exploring a knowledge graph with three tools, one call at a time, and every answer must cite edges the tools returned.

find(name)
up to ten nodes whose name matches, with type and connection count. Returns no edges.
expand(node, relation?, offset?)
the edges at a node in either direction, forty per page. A node with more than 150 connections returns a relation summary plus its forty most-connected edges.
connect(a, b, max_hops?)
up to three shortest paths between two nodes, as edges, three hops at most.

Seen means a tool returned it. The final answer is checked by set membership, never by a judge: it must cite at least one edge, every cited edge must have been seen, and the answer must name a node of a cited edge. Anything else is shown as an abstention with its reason. A benchmark question is also compared to its gold answer.

evidence, cited seen find, and new this turn connect answer cited but never seen

Prospector is Qwen3.5 after supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on its own traversals, served on this machine, with MLX on a Mac or vLLM on a GPU; the status strip names the size and the driver. The budget is the number of tool calls it is told it has; it was trained at 20.
